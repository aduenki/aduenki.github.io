Siderale — Privacy Policy

Effective 4 October 2026

Siderale shows how good the coming nights are for stargazing at the sites you save. This page says what leaves your phone, and it is short because little does.

When you add a site, its position rounded to about a kilometre is sent to Open-Meteo to fetch the forecast and the terrain height, and once to Apple's geocoder to find its time zone. If you choose the position on a map, the map tiles of the region you browse come from Apple's servers while the map is open, and your location is shown on it only to start the map where you are; it is not stored. Your sites stay on your phone.

Each forecast request carries the site's position rounded to about a kilometre and nothing else — no name, no account, no identifier. Haze and dust come from Open-Meteo's Air Quality API with the same rounded position. While a notification switch is on or a widget is placed, the app refreshes a few times a day in the background with that position.

Comets are read from the Minor Planet Center's public file; that request carries no position.

Your observing log, your gear, your sites and your settings stay on your phone; the log leaves it only when you export it yourself. The widget shows the night's score and times on your home and lock screen, never a position. There are no accounts, no analytics, no tracking, no advertising and no third-party software components.

Because nothing is collected, there is nothing to request, correct or delete. If a future version changes any of this, this page is updated before that version ships.
